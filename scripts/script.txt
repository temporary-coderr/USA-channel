You make a small mistake in public—and suddenly it feels like everyone noticed.

But most people are less focused on you than you imagine.

Psychologists call this the Spotlight Effect: overestimating how much others notice your appearance and behavior.

In a 2000 study, Thomas Gilovich, Victoria Medvec, and Kenneth Savitsky asked Cornell students to wear an embarrassing Barry Manilow T-shirt.

The wearers estimated about 48% of observers could identify the person pictured. In reality, only about 23% did.

Your mistake feels huge because you experienced it firsthand.

So next time you have an awkward moment, pause before assuming the whole room is judging you. Most people are busy thinking about themselves.

You're probably not the center of their attention—and that's freeing.
