You walk into a room, trip over a word, or say something awkward—and suddenly it feels like everyone noticed.

But most people are far less focused on you than you imagine.

Psychologists call this the Spotlight Effect: our tendency to overestimate how much other people notice our appearance and behavior.

In a 2000 study, Thomas Gilovich, Victoria Medvec, and Kenneth Savitsky asked Cornell students to wear an embarrassing Barry Manilow T-shirt before entering a room of other students.

The shirt-wearers estimated that about 48% of observers would be able to identify the person on the shirt.

But only about 23% actually did.

That doesn't mean nobody notices mistakes. It means your own experience feels more visible to you than it does to everyone else.

So when an awkward moment happens, pause before assuming the whole room is judging you. Most people are busy thinking about themselves.

You're probably not the center of their attention—and that's freeing.
